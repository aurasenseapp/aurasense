Step 1

Before you visit: 20 minutes online

Most surprises can be spotted from home. Open a map with satellite and street view, and look around the home for about a kilometre in every direction.

What makes noise nearby

  • Busy roads, highways, truck or bus routes
  • Railway lines or rail yards (trains often run at night)
  • An airport, and whether the home sits under its flight paths
  • Gravel pits, quarries, factories or industrial yards
  • Bars, venues, stadiums, schools, playgrounds or dog parks
  • Hospitals or fire stations (sirens)

What’s planned

  • Empty lots or old buildings nearby that could be rebuilt
  • The city or county website: development permits and planned construction near the address
  • Search the street name with “construction”, “noise” or “complaint”

Light and air

  • A light pollution map: how bright are the nights here?
  • Large signs, car lots or sports fields lit late at night
  • Farms, wastewater plants, restaurant exhaust or wood smoke nearby
  • Which way the wind usually blows, and what it carries

The building itself

  • Detached, or shared walls? Who is above, below and beside?
  • Where the bedrooms face: the street, or the quiet side?
  • The windows: single, double or triple pane? (Ask if the listing doesn’t say)
  • Parking lots, garbage bins or loading areas under the windows

Many airports publish maps of their flight paths. Many towns and counties publish maps of building permits.

Step 2

At the visit: use every sense

Slow down. A viewing is designed to show a place at its best; your senses are there to show you the rest.

Sound

  • Stand still for two minutes with the windows closed. What do you hear?
  • Open a bedroom window and listen again
  • Hums: fridge, furnace, air conditioning, pumps, power lines
  • Neighbours: footsteps, voices, TV, music, plumbing, doors
  • Knock gently on a shared wall. Does it sound hollow?
  • Record 30 seconds of sound on your phone in the empty bedroom, to compare later

Light

  • Where will streetlights, signs or headlights shine at night?
  • Neighbours’ security lights aimed at the windows?
  • Can the bedrooms be made fully dark?
  • How much daylight reaches the rooms you’ll use most?

Air and smell

  • Smoke, cannabis, cooking or chemical smells from other homes
  • Damp or mould: a musty smell, stains, peeling paint
  • Do the windows open? Does fresh air move through?
  • Scented candles or sprays can hide smells. Notice if they’re everywhere

Vibration

  • Feel the floor when a truck, bus or train passes
  • Lifts, laundry machines or garage doors on the other side of a wall

How it feels

  • After ten minutes, does your body feel calmer, or tenser than when you arrived?
  • Could you sleep well here?
  • Anything that felt wrong but you can’t yet name? Write it down anyway

Step 3

Come back, and ask

A place you viewed on a quiet Tuesday morning can be a different place on a Friday night. Even a short drive-by after dark tells you a lot.

VisitDay and timeWhat you noticed
1
2 · evening or weekend
3 · after dark

Ask the landlord or seller

  • Is any construction planned nearby, or on this street?
  • Have there been noise complaints here?
  • Is there a quiet-hours rule? Is smoking allowed in other units or on balconies?
  • What’s above, below and beside this home?
  • When do garbage pickup and snow clearing come by?
  • Why did the last people leave?

Ask a neighbour, if you can

  • “What’s it like living here? Is it quiet at night?”
  • “Anything you wish you’d known before you moved in?”
Write the answers down.
Answers given in person are easy to forget once you’ve fallen for a place. A note in your own hand keeps you honest with yourself.

Extra care

If sound, light or smell
affect you more than most

Light sleepers, people who work nights, and people who simply feel places deeply: these are for you.

  • Fewer shared walls means fewer surprises: a detached home, or a top-floor corner unit
  • Choose a bedroom on the side away from the street, the parking lot and the bins
  • Visit when the place is at its loudest, not only when it’s at its best
  • Keep headphones off during the visit, so you hear what you would live with
  • Bring someone you trust to notice what you might miss, and to notice how you seem
  • Ask whether you may come back once more, quietly, before you decide
  • Trust your first sense. If something felt wrong, it is worth a second look

What no viewing can show you

Some things only show up once you have lived somewhere for a whole week:

  • Smoke drifting in at 2 a.m., but only on certain nights
  • The garbage truck at 7:30 a.m. on two mornings every week
  • A car alarm locked three times beneath your window at midnight

The people who live there already know. On Aurasense, they share what a place is really like, any day and any hour. Once you move in, add what you notice, so the next person knows before they sign.
